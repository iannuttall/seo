[Dub](https://dub.co/) started as an open-source link shortener and is now a link attribution platform. It sells three things on one account: short links with analytics, conversion tracking from click to sale, and Dub Partners, which runs affiliate programs for SaaS companies such as Framer, Perplexity and Polymarket. Framer alone pays out about $900,000 a month through Dub Partners, and Dub says it has paid more than $20 million to partners.

The site has a Domain Rating of 82, higher than Rewardful (77) or PartnerStack (81). AI answers already cite Dub for four of the five link shortener questions I asked. And Dub owns two things it can build pages from: a Program Marketplace of 399 programs and the payout data behind them. That authority hasn't reached the affiliate pages yet, and that's where Dub could grow, so I lead with those pages.

First, none of the marketplace's pages is in dub.co's sitemap, which lists 259 addresses. Second, partners.dub.co has its own sitemap with 399 program pages, each naming itself as canonical, so every program is two equal pages on two hosts. Third, many program pages are thin: the Framer page has about 50 words, the Buffer page 53.

**The fix:** make dub.co/marketplace the one public home for every program, in line with keeping one domain. Point each partners.dub.co program page's canonical at its marketplace page and list only the marketplace in a sitemap. Then fill each program page from data Dub already holds: commission, how long it pays, cookie length, payout method and the category. A program page that answers "how much does the beehiiv affiliate program pay" in its first sentence is also the page an AI answer can quote.

The category pages carry the head terms. "Best AI SaaS affiliate programs in 2026" already exists at the AI category; it needs the programs ranked by a number a reader cares about, such as commission, with a short answer at the top.

The bigger link play is a report. Rewardful's "affiliate marketing statistics" page has links from 142 sites, and FirstPromoter's from 108, for a search with 2,400 US searches a month. Both are collections of other people's numbers. Dub has its own, from more than $20 million in payouts.

Even the public part is a story. I read the rewards line on all 399 program pages on partners.dub.co. Of the 292 that pay a share of each sale, the median is 25%, and the most common rates are 20% and 30%. About half the programs (196 of 387) stop paying after a year, and 81 pay for the customer's lifetime.

A headline a journalist could use: "The median SaaS affiliate program pays 25% of each sale, and half stop after a year (Dub, 387 programs, November 2026)." With the private data Dub could add what no one else can: which commission structures get the most partner sign-ups and sales, how much the top 1% of partners earn, and what share of clicks from creators turn into sales. The Polymarket payouts that went round X in October (one partner posted a notice for $7,439 in commissions) show the appetite for those numbers.

I'd publish it as a dated page at dub.co/affiliate-marketing-statistics, updated each quarter, and send it to the writers of the lists above and of the statistics pages that rank.

AI crawlers already get everything. GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot user agents get the same full pages a browser gets, and Dub's docs and help centre (on Mintlify) serve Markdown to the search bots. So the gaps are in what the pages say.

## Three technical fixes

The crawl (`seo crawl`, 600 pages each on dub.co and partners.dub.co) found plenty of small issues. Two of them matter, and a third comes from checking what dead short links return.

### 1. Made-up addresses on dub.co return the 404 page with a 200

Any unknown address at the top level of dub.co shows "404 Page not found" with a 200 status, so to Google each one is a working copy of a near-empty page. The blog, help and docs sections already return a real 404.

The tools index at dub.co/tools does the same: it's linked and its 17 tools are in the sitemap, but the index itself is that 404 page with a 200. The same catch-all serves llms-full.txt, pricing.md and partners.md as HTML.

**The fix:** call Next.js's `notFound()` in the catch-all route so it returns a 404 status, and give /tools a real index of the tools. To confirm it worked, request a made-up address and check for a 404.

### 2. The program pages compete with themselves

The partners.dub.co crawl found 398 duplicate titles, mostly each program's page against its apply page. The apply pages already point their canonical at the program page, so the duplicates that matter are the ones across the two domains in the marketplace section above. That fix covers this one.

### 3. Housekeeping: dead short links

The Indonesian visits in the traffic map land on the Links page, and a live Google search in Indonesia (DataForSEO, 5 November) puts that page first for "dugempoker", with its normal title and no gambling text anywhere on it. Ahrefs credits the page with one US visit a month. It started ranking in August, and it grew with its backlinks.

Indonesian gambling sites use Dub short links in their Telegram channels and landing pages, with link text like "SLOT GACOR" and "DAFTAR" (sign up). When one of those links stops existing, because it was deleted or never created, the dub.sh domain doesn't return a 404. Dub's code sends unknown links on its default domain to the Links page with a 307 redirect, and its own test suite checks for exactly that.

  after={{ label: "With a 404", html: afterRedirect }}

Ahrefs counts 41,167 links from 8,209 sites to the Links page, and the gambling anchors are among the most common ("toto slot" alone, 6,493 links from 248 sites). Google follows each redirect to the Links page, reads the link text, and ranks the page for the casino's name. Each new page that uses a dead Dub link adds another one.

I can't see inside Google, so this is the most likely cause rather than a proven one. The anchors, the redirect and the timing all point the same way. Either way, the Links page ranks for about 1,900 Indonesian searches, nearly all of them gambling brand names, that have nothing to do with the product.

**The fix:** return a 404 for a missing link on dub.sh, dub.link and the other Dub domains, marked noindex like the disabled and banned link pages. Keep the "Link not found" page with its link to Dub Links, so a person who clicks a dead link still sees the product. Branded domains that send unknown links to a tool page (chatg.pt goes to the ChatGPT link shortener) need the same change.

To confirm it worked, request a made-up dub.sh link and check for a 404, then watch the Links page's Indonesian keywords in Ahrefs or Search Console fall over the next few weeks. The US numbers shouldn't move.

## Get the changes seen quickly

The marketplace is the biggest test. Once the canonicals point at it, Google has to recrawl about 400 program pages on two hosts before it moves their signals across, and on its own schedule that can take weeks. I'd submit every marketplace page and every moved comparison to [Index My Site](https://indexmysite.app/) the day it ships, so Google crawls them straight away.

## How I ran this

Everything in this teardown came from SEO Skill, run by an agent. You can run the same audit on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free. You sign in with Google, and there's no password and nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web work too. The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for each one.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. The key gives your agent search volumes, rankings and results pages, like the tables above. With DataForSEO it can also ask ChatGPT and Google's AI answers your questions and check who they cite.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine, so it can read any site:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
Audit example.com and every domain we use for links.
Crawl each one with seo crawl, and check what unknown
addresses and short links return.
For our biggest page, check which countries and
searches its traffic comes from, and whether they
match what the page sells.
Use seo_research to find the searches our buyers make
and who ranks for them.
Ask ai_answers ten buyer questions for us and our top
competitor, and say who gets cited.
Rank the fixes by how much revenue each one is near.
```

On your own site, the hosted server adds what I couldn't see from the outside: your real Search Console clicks and queries. With those, you'd see the Indonesian impressions directly instead of through an estimate.

If you run a link shortener, check what your dead links return. Every redirect to your homepage or product page passes along whatever the linking page says about it.
