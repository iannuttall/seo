## 91% of Replit's Google traffic is brand search, and that's the strength to build on

Ahrefs estimates replit.com gets about 1,143,578 Google visits a month across all countries (5 November 2026). 931,754 of them (81%) land on the homepage from searches for Replit by name.

## Five compiler pages now redirect to the homepage, so the relaunch starts with a redirect map

Most relaunches lose rankings in the same few ways. Addresses change with no redirect, redirects go to the homepage, templates drop text or titles, and nobody compares before and after on the same pages. Each of those can be checked.

### Step 1: list every address that matters, from five sources

A sitemap alone misses most of what ranks and most of what's linked. I'd build one list from five places:

1. The sitemaps. Replit's [robots.txt](https://replit.com/robots.txt) lists six sitemaps, and between them they hold about 1,768 addresses (6 November 2026).
2. Pages with traffic: Search Console's top pages for the last 16 months, plus Ahrefs' top pages for anything Search Console anonymises.
3. Pages with links, meaning the target addresses from a backlink export. DataForSEO counts 58,574 referring domains to replit.com.
4. Old domains: repl.it (8,461 referring domains) and blog.replit.com (2,466), which already redirects post by post to [replit.com/blog](https://replit.com/blog).
5. Sections served from Replit apps. The careers, community and discover sections are served from Replit apps ([careers-site.replit.app](https://careers-site.replit.app/), [community-hub.replit.app](https://community-hub.replit.app/) and [discover-articles.replit.app](https://discover-articles.replit.app/discover/replit-vs-lovable)), each with a canonical pointing at replit.com. A relaunch that moves more sections this way needs the same canonical on each app.

### Step 2: map every old address to its closest new one

Every address on the list gets one of four outcomes. It stays, it gets a 301 to its closest new page, it returns a 404 on purpose, or it returns a 410 because it's gone for good. Nothing redirects to the homepage unless it was the homepage.

### Step 3: record the before numbers

The week before launch, I'd save four things to compare against:

- A full crawl with `seo crawl https://replit.com/ --max-pages 10000`, saved as JSON. It records each page's status, title, canonical, robots tags, word count and internal links.
- Search Console clicks by page and query for the 28 days before launch.
- AI answers to the same 10 buyer questions from this post, run through `seo research ai_answers`, so the AI citation count has a baseline too.
- The top 200 pages by clicks, with their titles and H1s copied out. Titles change by accident in most redesigns.

### Step 4: check on launch day and for 30 days after

On launch day, every old address from step 1 goes through the crawler in list mode, and every redirect gets traced:

```sh
seo crawl --mode list --urls-file old-urls.txt --concurrency 2
seo redirect-trace --url https://replit.com/languages/python3
seo compare --site replit.com --date 2026-12-01
```

The list crawl shows every old address that now returns a 404 or a redirect chain. `seo redirect-trace` follows one address hop by hop and checks the final page is indexable. `seo compare --date` splits Search Console into equal periods before and after the launch date and lists the pages and queries that moved, so a lost page shows up by name instead of as a dip in a chart.

## YouTube was a source in 15 of 30 AI answers, and Zapier in 9

Replit's site makes the claim. What AI answers repeat comes from what other sites say about it. Across the 30 answers about Replit in the same run, YouTube was a source in 15, Zapier in 9, Lovable's site in 8, and Bolt's and Reddit in 6 each.

LinkedIn was a source in none of them. That fits the published studies. [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/linkedin-ai-visibility-study) found LinkedIn in about 11% of AI answers across ChatGPT Search, Perplexity and AI Mode (January and February 2026), and [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/linkedin-is-the-most-cited-domain-for-professional-queries-in-ai-search) found it the most cited domain for professional questions. Replit's business pitch, internal tools and company-wide agents, is the professional question, and that's where LinkedIn posts would count.

### One message, repeated everywhere answers look

AI answers repeat what many sources say in the same words. Replit's homepage title says "AI App and Website Builder". Two recent customer posts on its [X account](https://x.com/Replit), about [a support team's triage app](https://x.com/Replit/status/2117339099468341633) (2 November 2026) and [a Japanese leasing company](https://x.com/Replit/status/2118584164723331526) (6 November 2026), don't say "app builder", "website builder" or "vibe coding".

I'd pick one line for each audience and use it on every surface for 90 days:

- **For builders:** "Describe your app. Replit builds it, hosts it and publishes it." That's the homepage, the use-case pages, YouTube titles and X posts.
- **For companies:** "Every team can build its own internal tools on Replit." That's the business pages, LinkedIn posts and the customer stories.

Then each piece of work goes out five ways. One customer story, such as the support team that built its own ticket triage app, becomes a page on replit.com with numbers, a YouTube walkthrough with the same title, a LinkedIn post from the person who built it, an X thread that links the page, and an answer in the Reddit threads that ask how to build that tool.

## Six technical fixes, closest to revenue first

Replit already keeps user apps off its main domain. They're published on replit.app, its [template repls](https://replit.com/@replit/Python) carry a noindex tag, and a deleted user's repl returns a 404. That's the right split, and it means these fixes are all about Replit's own pages.

### 1. repl.it redirects with a 302, and 8,461 sites link to it

Replit [moved from repl.it to replit.com in March 2021](https://replit.com/blog/dotcom), and the old domain still has 8,461 referring domains (DataForSEO, 6 November 2026). Every address on it redirects to the same path on replit.com with a 302, the code for a temporary move. Google can come to treat a long-standing redirect as permanent, so I can't show from outside that any link value is lost. It's a risk, and removing it is a one-line change.

<BeforeAfter

  before={{ label: "Today", html: beforeReplIt }}
  after={{ label: "After the fix", html: afterReplIt }}
/>

**The fix:** change the rule at the edge from 302 to 301, keeping the path. To confirm it worked, run `seo redirect-trace --url https://repl.it/languages/python3` and check the first hop says 301.

### 2. The sitemaps list 87 addresses Google can't index, and 9 of 14 child sitemaps list nothing

The [main sitemap](https://replit.com/sitemaps/main.xml) lists 105 addresses. 57 are /languages addresses. Of those, 55 redirect to the homepage, /languages redirects to /templates and /templates/languages returns a 404. Another 30 are template repls that carry a noindex tag. In the second sitemap index, the careers, enterprise and partners children return a 404, and five more (growth-kit, startups, community, racetorevenue and industries/government) return an HTML page. Those 87 addresses add noise to the one file meant to say what should be indexed. I can't see from outside whether it slows Google's crawling, so it's a risk, not a measured loss.

<BeforeAfter
  caption="Replit's sitemaps on 6 November 2026, and what they should hold. Counts are from the live files."
  before={{ label: "Today", html: beforeSitemap }}
  after={{ label: "After the fix", html: afterSitemap }}
/>

**The fix:** generate the sitemaps from the same list as the redirect map, so only live, indexable, self-canonical pages go in. Remove the three 404 children and the empty one from the index, and serve XML for the five that return HTML. Joost de Valk found the same pattern on [most big sites he checked](https://joost.blog/broken-sitemaps), where the sitemap is built by a different system from the pages. To confirm it worked, run `seo crawl --sitemap-url https://replit.com/sitemap.xml --health` and check that every listed address returns 200.

### 3. 8,449 linking domains point at Replit pages that return a 404

DataForSEO counts 8,449 referring domains whose link to replit.com lands on a 404 (6 November 2026). Most go to deleted user repls, which should stay gone. The rest go to sections Replit retired itself, such as the old forum at /talk, /repls addresses, /tutorials and old docs paths. A link from [a GitHub blog post on GitHub Classroom](https://github.blog/developer-skills/github-education/code-in-the-browser-with-github-classroom/) to a Git tutorial on the old forum is one of them.

**The fix:** add the retired sections to the redirect map from step 2, by pattern. Send /talk threads to the community site or the docs page on the same topic, and old docs paths to their new docs pages. To confirm it worked, crawl the list of linked 404 targets in list mode and count the ones that now resolve.

### 4. Sign-in addresses with parameters are indexed

Ahrefs credits a sign-in address with about 1,113 visits a month for "replit login". The address carries a private deployment flag and a redirect to someone's app in its query string. The plain [login page](https://replit.com/login)'s title starts with "Sign Up", and it has no canonical tag.

**The fix:** add a canonical on the login and sign-up pages that points to the address without parameters, and a noindex on any sign-in address that carries a redirect. Fix the login page title to say "Log in". To confirm it worked, inspect the parameter address in Search Console after a recrawl and check Google picked the plain address as canonical.

### 5. llms.txt lists 228 docs pages and one product page

[replit.com/llms.txt](https://replit.com/llms.txt) is a copy of the docs index, with 228 docs pages and one product link, to Replit Slides. Nothing in it says what Replit is or links the use-case, pricing or comparison pages.

### 6. The docs speak five languages, and the marketing site speaks one

Replit's docs serve English, French, Portuguese, Japanese and Korean with matching hreflang tags, as on the [Japanese Agent overview](https://docs.replit.com/ja/features/agent/overview). The marketing site has no localised pages. Both replit.com/ja and replit.com/es return a 404, and the use-case pages carry no hreflang.

Replit's latest customer story, [posted on X on 6 November 2026](https://x.com/Replit/status/2118584164723331526), is a Japanese leasing company. I'd start with Japanese copies of the two use-case pages and the pricing page, with hreflang between the English and Japanese versions, and measure them before doing more languages.

## Get the changes seen quickly

A relaunch changes thousands of addresses at once, and Google gets round to them on its own schedule. I'd submit every new and changed address to [Index My Site](https://indexmysite.app/) on launch day, so Google is asked to crawl it straight away. That matters most for the merged comparison pages and the redirected compiler pages, where nothing settles until Google has seen the new version.

## The lesson for any relaunch

Write the redirect map before you build the new site. The pages that rank are rarely the ones a redesign is planned around, and the cheapest fix in this teardown is a 302 that should have been a 301.

## How I ran this

An agent ran this teardown with SEO Skill for keyword data and AI answers, Ahrefs for traffic estimates, and DataForSEO's API for the live results pages and link counts. Replit's Cloudflare setup challenges unknown crawlers, SEO Skill's included, so I checked its pages one request at a time. You can run the same checks on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free. You sign in with Google, and there's no password and nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web work too. The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for each one.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. The key gives your agent search volumes, results pages and AI answers, like the tables above.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine, so it can read any site:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
We're relaunching example.com. Build a URL inventory
from our sitemaps, our top pages and our backlink
targets, and check each one with seo crawl in list mode.
Trace every redirect with seo redirect-trace and flag
any that end on the homepage.
Ask ai_answers ten buyer questions for us and for our
biggest competitor, and list the pages each answer cites.
After launch, run seo compare with the launch date.
```

On your own site, the hosted server adds what I couldn't see from outside. That's your real Search Console clicks by page, before and after the launch date.
