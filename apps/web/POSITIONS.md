# Positions

Ian's settled stances on SEO and AEO, as seoskill.dev posts and pages state
them. Every post must agree with this file. Read it before you write or edit
a post, and check each claim in your draft against it.

- A post that needs a stance not listed here: add it, with its evidence and
  exceptions, in the same PR, and ask Ian when it's a judgement call.
- A changed stance: update every post listed under it in the same PR.
- Keep each entry to the stance, the evidence, the exceptions and the posts.

## AI answers cite search results, and recommend from training

When ChatGPT cites a page, it's almost always one its own search found (88%
of cited pages came from its general search results in Ahrefs' study of 1.4
million prompts). Many answers cite nothing: definitions, well-known facts
and a lot of brand recommendations come from the model's training. The way
into those is brand mentions next to the topic and clear entity work, which
pays off slowly.

- Never say AI answers use search results "and nothing else".
- Posts: how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt, query-fan-out, aeo-vs-seo.

## Years go in titles only for time-sensitive pages, never in URLs

Put the year in the title when the page covers something that changes each
year (prices, rankings, rules) and update it when the year turns. An
out-of-date year in a title does more harm than none. Keep years out of URLs,
so the page keeps its address and links when it's updated. ChatGPT and
Gemini add years to many of their searches.

- Posts: how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt, query-fan-out.

## Self-ranking "best X" lists work today, done credibly

They get cited: when ChatGPT cites a software company's own site for a
buying question, it's a "best" list on the company's blog about a third of
the time (Ahrefs). They may not keep working. The credible version tests
each option, says who it suits and ranks a competitor first when it's
better. A list that puts the publisher first every time is spam.

- Posts: how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt, aeo-vs-seo.

## AEO is mostly SEO

AI engines cite what their search finds and recommend what their training
learned, so the work is SEO plus brand mentions. Say AEO, not GEO. AEO spam
is old SEO spam on new platforms.

- Posts: aeo-vs-seo, how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt, query-fan-out.

## One page per question group, never a page per fan-out query

Cover related questions as sections on the page that already ranks. Google's
guide calls a page for every fan-out query scaled content abuse.

- Posts: query-fan-out, how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt, keyword-clustering.

## llms.txt and schema don't win AI citations

Google Search ignores llms.txt, and OpenAI's search docs don't mention it. It
can help agents read docs, which is why teardowns may recommend it. Adding
schema didn't increase AI citations in Ahrefs' study; keep it for Google's
rich results and for making clear who you are.

- Posts: how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt, aeo-vs-seo, teardowns.

## AI agents are a growing way people search, as Ian's opinion

Search still sends far more visits than AI today. Ian expects AI agents to
do most of the informational searching SEO has targeted within the next two
to three years. Present the shift as real and growing, and the timeline as
his view, not a finding. Never present the fall in visits per person
(SparkToro and Similarweb) as proof that AI caused it.
- Posts: aeo-vs-seo, search-ctr-by-position, indexnow, keyword-clustering,
  unlinked-brand-mentions, bing-long-tail, ai-mode-follow-up-questions.

## Google's terms for Google's data

Use Google's own wording for its data, such as "anonymized queries" for the
searches Search Console leaves out for privacy, and "follow-up question" for
AI Mode. Introduce the term once in plain words.

- Posts: bing-long-tail, ai-mode-follow-up-questions.
