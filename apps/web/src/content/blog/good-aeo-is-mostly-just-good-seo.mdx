Every time someone walks me through the new AEO playbook, I recognise it. You get mentioned on other sites, answer the question properly, cover the follow-ups, show up on Reddit and YouTube, and make it obvious who you are.

That's an SEO plan, and it was one in 2014 too. Good SEOs were selling it long before anyone called it AEO or GEO.

I don't mind the new name much. What bugs me is the pitch that it's a new discipline, with a new budget, that your SEO team can't do. Most of it is the work they've been asking to do for years. Plenty of very experienced SEOs, [Ian Howells](https://x.com/ianhowells/status/2120701494248812838) among them, say the same.

Google says the same. Its [guide to its AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says optimizing for them "is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." AI Overviews and AI Mode pull their sources from Google's search index, so that's simply how they work.

## AEO tactics SEOs were already using

Go through any AEO checklist and most items have an obvious older version.

**Answer the question first.** Dr. Pete wrote this up for featured snippets [back in 2016](https://moz.com/blog/ranking-zero-seo-for-answers). Rank on page one, then lead with "a quick summary at the top, followed by a deeper dive." The only new advice is to chop pages into tiny chunks for LLMs, and [Google's own AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says you don't need to. Clear sections that each answer one question, with the answer up top, still help any engine pull the right snippet.

**Cover the questions people ask next.** Query fan-out means the AI runs a handful of extra searches for the follow-up questions, so your page has to answer more than the headline query. We used to call that covering search intent, and AJ Kohn put it well in 2015: "Target the keyword, optimize the intent."

**Get people talking about you.** Rand Fishkin did a Whiteboard Friday [in 2012](https://moz.com/blog/prediction-anchor-text-is-dying-and-will-be-replaced-by-cocitation-whiteboard-friday) about Google noticing brands that get mentioned next to a topic, even without a link. Now Ahrefs finds [brand mentions line up with AI visibility](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation) far more than backlinks do do.

**Be where your customers already talk.** The old BlueHatSEO [e-commerce checklist from 2010](https://www.bluehatseo.com/seo-checklist-for-e-commerce-sites/) has most of it. Join the forums in your niche and answer product questions every week, put your products in Google's shopping feed, film reviews and post them on the video sites. Swap in Reddit, Merchant Center and YouTube and you've got a 2026 AEO retainer.

**Get onto the lists that get cited.** The "citation gap" report every AEO tool sells means checking which pages the AI cites and getting your brand onto them. That's link building from your competitors' backlinks, which SEOs have always done.

**Make it obvious who you are.** Clear About page, consistent name and description everywhere, structured data, a fair Wikipedia entry if you've earned one. AJ Kohn wrote the checklist in 2014 and called it [Knowledge Graph Optimization](https://www.blindfiveyearold.com/knowledge-graph-optimization). His rule still holds: "Write clearly so that both humans and search engines know what the hell you're talking about."

And the basic one: let the crawlers in. OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity don't publish ranking guides. What they do publish is "don't block our search bot". That's robots.txt, which is about as old as SEO gets. As for the newer llms.txt file, Google's AI guide says you can ignore it for Google Search.

## AEO spam is old SEO spam on new platforms

The shady AEO tactics are the shady SEO tactics with new platform names.

Fake Reddit accounts are the 2026 version of spamming Yahoo Answers. "Best X" lists that always put the publisher at number one are voting for yourself on a top sites list. A page for every fan-out query is the million-page database site. Planting brand facts on Wikipedia is, well, planting links on Wikipedia.

They died in Google for the same reason they'll die here. Reddit's share of ChatGPT answers has already dropped hard this year, and Google's AI guide calls mass-produced fan-out pages scaled content abuse. The tactics that lasted were the ones that left something real behind, like a question actually answered, a review someone actually filmed, a product feed that's actually accurate.

## Where AEO and SEO actually differ

**There's no rank to track.** Ask an AI the same question twice and you'll often get a different list of brands. SparkToro [ran this at scale](https://www.sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) and found the same list came back less than once in a hundred tries. So anyone selling you an "AI ranking position" is selling something that doesn't exist. You measure by asking the same questions many times and counting how often you show up.

**People don't type keywords.** Prompts are long and personal. The engine turns them into its own searches that you can't see, so your keyword research is now a guess at what the machine will search for.

**Other people's pages matter more.** AI answers lean harder on reviews, lists, forums and video than the blue links ever did. More of the work happens off your site.

**Each engine is its own thing.** ChatGPT, Google's AI Mode, Perplexity and Claude pull from different places. Ranking well in Google doesn't mean ChatGPT will mention you, so you check each one.

None of that adds up to a new set of tactics, though. It's a new way of measuring, plus more weight on stuff SEOs already did.

## AI assistants still send far less traffic than search

Across 118,671 sites in Ahrefs' [October 2026 data](https://ahrefs.com/benchmarks/ai-vs-search), AI assistants sent 0.46% of visits. Search engines sent 27.55%, about 60 times as many.

AI visitors do seem to convert better, and showing up in an answer can shape what people buy even when nobody clicks. So I'd track it. I just wouldn't move budget out of SEO to chase it, especially since the SEO work is what gets you into the answers in the first place.

## What I'd do instead of buying AEO

Here's where I'd put the time and money:

1. Check robots.txt and your CDN aren't blocking Googlebot, Bingbot or the AI search bots.
2. Find the questions your buyers actually ask, and make sure a page answers each one in its first sentence and covers the follow-ups.
3. Get talked about in the places they hang out, like the review sites, the lists, the subreddits and the YouTube reviews, using real accounts and a product people actually like.
4. Make your About page and profiles say clearly who you are and what you do.
5. Once a month, ask the AI engines your 10 most important questions a few times each and note how often you come up.

The first four are what SEOs have been asking for budget to do for a decade. If you've been doing them all along, you're already doing AEO. Tell whoever's pitching you exactly that.
