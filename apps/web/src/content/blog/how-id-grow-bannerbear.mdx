[Bannerbear](https://www.bannerbear.com/) turns templates into images, videos and PDFs through an API. You design a template once, send it text and photos, and get back a social post, a product banner, a certificate or a subtitled video. It sells an Image API, a Video API, a PDF API, a Workflow API that chains them together, and an MCP server so agents can use your templates. Plans start at $49 a month. Jon Yongfook built it bootstrapped and wrote about the climb from zero to $10k MRR and beyond on the site.

Bannerbear already gets more search traffic than the API companies it's compared with, and it has added 22 free tools that run in the browser, for video, images and PDFs, the ones I checked since August. That's the upside I'd build on.

No product page, pricing page or comparison has an estimated visit from US search. Searches for the brand land on the homepage, and Bannerbear ranks first for its own name.

I'd stop writing posts about other companies' AI tools. The Bing Image Creator post has links from 249 sites, more than any page but the homepage, and the Cursor and Claude Code posts bring visits. But the people reading them came to install an editor or try an image model, and nothing on those pages moves them toward a template.

Jon posted this week that Bannerbear's Google traffic has been going down all year and has started to turn after a week of changes. From outside I can't see that history, so this teardown is about the pages that work today and the ones that could.

## The certificate generator already works, and it should lead to the bulk workflow

The free certificate generator is Bannerbear's best page for search. It gets about 1,157 estimated US visits a month from 279 searches, ranks first for "certificate generator" and "certificate creator", and has links from 125 sites. It has been live since at least 2021.

Some of those searches are for fake diplomas, and those visitors were never going to buy. The ones who matter are making certificates for a class, a course or an event, and the next thing they need is the same certificate for 40 names.

Bannerbear has built exactly that. Its workflow "Print Certificates and Wallet Cards from a Spreadsheet" turns a list of people into a print-ready PDF pack. The certificate generator never mentions it. It talks about bulk twice: a sentence near the top with no link, and an FAQ answer that sends people to the PDF API page, where they'd still have to build the job themselves.

**The fix:** link that paragraph to the spreadsheet workflow, and put the same link under the download button, where someone who just made one certificate is looking. Then do the same on the other four generators (invoice, collage, event ID card, wedding invite), each pointing at the workflow or API that makes the same thing in bulk.

I'd leave "certificate of completion template" (1,900 US searches) and "award certificate template" (2,400) alone. Adobe, Canva and Pinterest fill those results, and the generator already ranks first for most of the "generator" searches and in the top five for "certificate maker", which come from people who want the finished file.

## Bannerbear's new free tools need the names people search for

Bannerbear's 22 browser tools include a media converter, video compressor, subtitle tool, background remover, Markdown to PDF and more. The ones I checked first appear in the Wayback Machine in August 2026, so they're new. Each page explains the job, shows the steps and pitches the API, with a line such as "Want to convert, resize, watermark or generate media automatically at scale?" That line links to the homepage, so I'd point each one at the Video, Image or PDF API page that does the job. The pages don't rank yet for the searches below, each a job one of Bannerbear's free tools already does: the Media Converter for the first three, then the Markdown to PDF converter, the Facebook and LinkedIn preview tools and the subtitle tool.

### Give each format conversion its own page

The Media Converter handles MP4, WebM, MOV, GIF, MP3 and more on one page titled "Free Online Media Converter". Nobody searches for that. They search for the pair they need: "mov to mp4" (60,500 a month, difficulty 13), "webm to mp4" (22,200, difficulty 7), "mp4 to gif" (110,000, difficulty 30). The results for those are single-purpose converter pages, and Templated, the closest rival in search, ranks with exactly that kind of page, such as its AVIF to JPG converter.

Each pair is a different job with a different file, so I'd give the biggest ones their own page: the same converter, preset to that pair, with a title, a first paragraph and an FAQ about that format. Start with MOV to MP4, WebM to MP4 and MP4 to GIF, then add pairs from the searches they start to collect.

### Name the official tool in the title

The X card preview tool is the only tool page with more than a handful of estimated visits, about 268 a month from the US, and it ranks first for "twitter card preview" and "check twitter card". Its title names the job and X's own tool, "Card Validator". The LinkedIn and Facebook tools do the same job, and their titles don't say what people call it. LinkedIn's own tool is the Post Inspector, and people search "linkedin post inspector" 1,600 times a month at a difficulty of 6. Facebook's is the Sharing Debugger, and "facebook debugger" gets 6,600.

### Build the tools index

Every tool lives under bannerbear.com/tools/, and that address returns a 404, as do /tools/image/ and /tools/pdf/. Only the video tools have an index page. A /tools/ page that lists every tool by job gives each one a link from a page about tools, and gives people who land on one tool an easy way to find the next.

All three engines cite the pricing page for "How much does Bannerbear cost?", and the alternatives page and the Bannerbear vs Placid post were each cited for choice questions. For "What are the best Bannerbear alternatives?", ChatGPT named Bannerbear without a link in Bannerbear's run and linked placid.app in Placid's. Only AI Mode cited Bannerbear's alternatives page, which is the page that should win that question on every engine.

The job questions are the gap. "How do I generate PDF certificates in bulk from a spreadsheet?" and "How do I add subtitles to videos automatically with an API?" got no Bannerbear citation or mention in any of the six answers, and none again when I asked both a second time. The answers cited Certifier, DocsAutomator and YouTube for certificates, and OpenAI, Deepgram and Shotstack for subtitles. Bannerbear sells both: the spreadsheet workflow above, and subtitles in its Video API.

GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot user agents get the same full pages a browser gets, and robots.txt welcomes each one by name, so access doesn't explain the gap. Bannerbear has pages near both questions, such as an FFmpeg subtitles tutorial and the spreadsheet workflow, but none is titled with the question and answers it with Bannerbear's own product.

**The fix:** a page titled with each question, with the answer in its first paragraph and the steps below it, that ranks for the search:

- "How to generate certificates in bulk from a spreadsheet", as the spreadsheet workflow page itself or a guide that links to it.
- "How to add subtitles to videos automatically with an API", in the Video API docs, with the request and the result, linked from the FFmpeg tutorial.
- "How to automatically generate Open Graph images for your blog". Only the AI Overview named Bannerbear for this one, without a link, and the answers cite Vercel and Next.js docs. Bannerbear already has "3 Ways to Generate Open Graph Images Automatically" and a Next.js guide, so I'd fold them into one page with that title and working code for each framework, and redirect the rest of the set to it.

## Finish the move to the V5 site

Bannerbear launched V5 this year with a new site template, and about half the site hasn't moved. Of the 1,402 addresses in the sitemap, 651 still render the old template, with the old menu ("Multi Image API", "Twitter to Instagram Generator Tool") next to 740 pages on the new one.

The old pages include all five generators, the template library (320 pages), 85 use-case pages, the old integrations, demos and solutions pages, and 201 help pages. Help, demos and solutions have V5 versions now. The generators, templates and most use cases don't.

### Two help centres answer the same questions

The help centre at bannerbear.com/help says "Guides and answers for Bannerbear V5" and links the V5 articles under /v5/help/. The old articles are still live at /help/articles/, 195 of them in the sitemap, each pointing its canonical at itself. Some have a V5 twin with the same title.

### Housekeeping

- The template pages share one paragraph of copy, and 14 templates are published twice under two IDs, such as the Certificate of Completion template. Their titles start "Auto Generate Images / Videos from the" with a double space before the name.
- The sitemap lists five sample pages marked noindex, under /sample_media/, and old /product/ addresses that redirect. List only the final 200 pages.
- Three internal links point at 404s: an old QR code product page, a podcast clip template and /v5/integrations/.
- /tools/shot/ has no title before the brand name, no heading, and nothing links to it.
- The old Video API address under /product/ redirects to the Workflow API page. Point it at the Video API page.

## Get the changes seen quickly

Google will find new tool pages on its own, but the redirects only pay off once it recrawls the old addresses, and there are about 200 of them in the help centre alone. I'd submit the new pages, the renamed tools and every redirected address to [Index My Site](https://indexmysite.app/) the day they ship, so Google is asked to crawl them straight away.

## How I ran this

Everything in this teardown came from SEO Skill, run by an agent, plus hand checks with curl, the Wayback Machine and Jon's public X posts. You can run the same audit on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free. You sign in with Google, and there's no password and nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web work too. The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for each one.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. The key gives your agent search volumes, rankings and results pages, like the tables above. With DataForSEO it can also ask ChatGPT and Google's AI answers your questions and check who they cite.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine, so it can read any site:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
seo crawl https://example.com/ --max-pages 1500
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
Audit example.com for US search and AI answers.
Group our estimated search traffic by kind of page
and tell me which pages sell something.
For each free tool, find the searches people use
for that job, with volume and who ranks.
Crawl the whole sitemap and tell me which sections
still use an old template or have a second copy.
Ask ai_answers ten questions about our product,
the jobs it does and which tool to choose, for us
and our closest rival, and say who gets cited.
Rank the fixes by how close each is to a sale.
```

On your own site, the hosted server adds your real Search Console clicks and queries, which I couldn't see from outside. With those you'd see which tool searches already bring impressions, and the trend this teardown can't.
