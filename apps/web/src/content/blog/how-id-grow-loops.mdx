[Loops](https://loops.so/) is email for software companies. Marketing campaigns, product and lifecycle emails, and transactional email like password resets all come from one contact record, set up by a marketer in the editor or by a developer through the API. It's free up to 1,000 contacts, and the Growth plan starts at $49 a month, priced by subscribed contacts with unlimited sends on paid plans. Chris Frantz and Adam Kaczmarek started it in 2022 through Y Combinator.

People already look for it by name. The homepage ranks first for "loops email", "loops login" and "loops email marketing". Earlier this month Chris posted that October was Loops' best month yet for new paid users, with growth still coming entirely from word of mouth. Search is the obvious second channel, for the people who need a SaaS email tool and haven't heard of Loops yet.

The upside is that most of the work is done. Since July, Loops has added 33 guides and 8 free tools, on top of 18 integration pages, 32 email templates and 16 email example pages. Google ranks a lot of them, mostly on pages two to five. Here's how I'd move them up, and the comparisons with Resend, SendGrid, Customer.io and Postmark I'd add, since Loops has no page for those searches yet.

The glossary visits are mostly people looking up a word, and I'd leave them alone. They don't hurt, and the glossary is where the site's search visitors already are, which makes it the best place to link from (more on that below).

DataForSEO estimates the 33 guides and 8 tools at about 25 visits a month between them. That's a floor, since it only counts searches in DataForSEO's database and misses the long tail, so Loops' Search Console will show more. The guides are four months old and most of the tools a few weeks, and Google already ranks the guides for 73 searches and the tools for 88, and 148 of those rankings sit between positions 11 and 50. My read is that pages in that range are past the hard part, since Google has placed them, and what they need most now is links, from inside the site and from other sites. Some also need to use the words people search with, as the next sections show.

One caveat on the examples. DataForSEO's ranking data puts the waitlist examples page second for "waitlist email" (5,400 US searches a month) and credits it with about 232 visits. When I checked the live results on 14 November, on desktop and on mobile, there was no Loops result I could identify in the top 16. Loops' own Search Console will say whether that page has slipped, and it's the first thing I'd look at.

## Page-two searches Loops' pages don't name

Some of the cheapest changes on a site like this are on pages that already sit just off the first page of Google for a search, where the page never uses the words people searched. Google has decided the page is relevant. Using the searcher's words where a reader would expect them, in a heading or a section that answers what they asked, is often the nudge that moves it up. I'd never stuff a phrase in for its own sake. Each change should make the page better for the person who searched.

From outside, DataForSEO puts Loops' pages at positions 11 to 20 for at least 149 searches with 50 or more US searches a month. Many are dictionary lookups for "recipient", which I'd leave. I compared the rest with each page's title, main heading and text, and a few stand out.

### Treat every tool page as a landing page

A tool ranks when the page around it answers what the person needs. That means what the check does, how to read the result, the problems it solves, what it can't tell you, and what to do next, which is where Loops comes in. Most of Loops' tool pages already carry about 670 to 1,000 words of that. Two don't. The subject line tester has about 400 words, and the UTM generator about 390. I'd bring those up to the standard of the mailto page, and build every new checker below to it from day one.

### Two more tools Loops can build from what it has

- **Email header analyzer** (3,600 searches, difficulty 33; MxToolbox and Trustifi rank). The EML viewer page already explains Received lines and authentication headers, and the viewer opens the files they come from. A page that takes pasted headers and shows the route, the SPF, DKIM and DMARC results and the delays between hops is the same parser with a different input.
- **Blacklist checker** (2,900 searches, difficulty 0; small tool sites rank). It's a set of DNS lookups against the public blocklists, the same kind of query the DNS checker already runs, and it answers the question every sender asks when mail stops arriving.
