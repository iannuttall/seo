Search Console tells you what happens in Google before the click. Your analytics tells you what happens after it.

## Where each tool works

| Tool | Hosted MCP | CLI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Analytics | Yes, with your Google sign-in | Yes, `seo analytics google report` |
| Clicky | Yes, with the site saved on your account page | Yes, `seo analytics clicky report` |
| Fathom, Simple Analytics, Plausible or anything else | No | Build your own from the [provider template](https://github.com/iannuttall/seo-provider-template) |

The hosted MCP reads your Google data, your Clicky sites and your DataForSEO or Ahrefs research. When you ask it about another analytics tool, or about [Bing](/docs/bing), it tells your agent to use the CLI, the same way it does for a crawl.

## Google Analytics

**Hosted MCP.** Your Google sign-in already allows read-only Google Analytics access. Ask your agent:

```text
Show the Google Analytics search landing pages for example.com
```

The agent runs `seo_data` with Google Analytics as the source and the numeric property ID, which `seo_sites` lists. It returns landing pages for sessions in the Organic Search channel, for the last 28 days unless you ask for other dates, with sessions, users, engaged sessions and key events. The result says when Google Analytics hid small rows or grouped some into "(other)".

**CLI.** `seo start` signs you in with Google. List your properties to find the numeric property ID, then pass it to a report:

```sh
seo analytics google properties
seo analytics google report --property 123456789
```

It returns the same landing pages as the MCP tool.

## Clicky

Add Clicky on your [account page](/account#clicky), under **Keys**, in one of two ways:

- **Your whole Clicky account.** Enter your Clicky username or email and your Clicky password. Every site in the account is added, and sites you add to Clicky later appear the first time an agent asks for them. The password is encrypted and kept for this, so use a Clicky password you use nowhere else. It does not work if your Clicky account uses two-factor sign-in or Sign in with Google. Use one site at a time then.
- **One site.** Enter its site ID and sitekey, which Clicky shows under Preferences, then Info. The sitekey is checked with Clicky, encrypted and never shown again. Add as many sites as you need.

To stop, remove the account or the site on the account page. Changing your Clicky password also stops a saved account login from working.

**Hosted MCP.** Ask your agent:

```text
Show the Clicky landing pages for example.com
```

The agent runs `seo_data` with Clicky as the source and the site's domain or its Clicky site ID. `seo_sites` lists the saved Clicky sites. It returns visits by landing page, with the traffic sources and search engines that sent visits, for the last 28 days unless you ask for other dates. Clicky reads at most 31 days at a time.

**CLI.** Link the CLI to your account with `seo login`, then:

```sh
seo analytics clicky report --site example.com
```

A Clicky site saved on this machine only with `seo analytics clicky connect --site-id 12345` works too, by its site ID.

Clicky counts visits, not Google Analytics sessions or users, so compare the two with care. Clicky does not split landing pages by source, so the landing pages include every visit, not only visits from search.

## Any other analytics tool

There is no built-in support for Fathom, Simple Analytics, Plausible or any other analytics tool, and there will be no more built-in sources. You can add your own in about an hour: copy the [provider template](https://github.com/iannuttall/seo-provider-template), point it at your tool's API and publish it to npm. Then:

```sh
seo providers install <your-package-name>
seo providers connect <provider-id>
```

`seo` shows the package's details and asks before it installs. It saves the key, limits network use and keeps your tool's numbers apart from Google's. A provider package runs in the CLI and the local MCP server, not on the hosted server.

For one page's queries, use [`seo page`](/docs/page). For raw Search Console rows, use [`seo data`](/docs/data).
