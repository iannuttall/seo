[Monologue](https://www.monologue.to/) is a dictation app for Mac, iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, built by [Every](https://every.to/). You hold a key, talk, and polished text lands in whatever app you're using. It costs $15 a month, or it comes free with an Every subscription.

Monologue's parent company, Every, has a Domain Rating of 77. The search traffic is low because its pages target the wrong searches, and because Every's links all go to the homepage.

Some of that gap is brand. About 80% of Wispr Flow's traffic is people typing "wispr flow" (39,000 US searches a month, and another 23,000 who spell it "whisper flow"). You can't SEO your way into someone else's brand demand.

Smaller apps like Willow Voice and Spokenly pull in thousands of visits from people who have never heard of them, searching for things like "dictation software" and "speech to text mac". Visitors like these are the ones most likely to start a trial.

## Every visit Monologue gets comes from one word

Every keyword Monologue ranks for in Ahrefs is the word "monologue", in one country or another. All of it lands on the homepage. DataForSEO agrees: 49 of the 50 searches it has Monologue ranking for go to the homepage.

That word is mostly searched by people looking for theatre monologues. So when Google tested Monologue for it, traffic rose. When Google decided those searchers wanted drama school, it fell.

- five use-case pages (developers, writers, product managers, founders and support)
- a dictation page, a notes page, pricing, docs and a changelog

## The pages answer a question buyers don't ask

The comparison pages are titled "Monologue vs Wispr Flow", "Monologue vs Superwhisper" and so on. It's a natural way to write them, but almost nobody searches for those titles, because you can only search "Monologue vs Wispr Flow" if you already know Monologue exists.

About 1,300 people a month search for a Wispr Flow alternative, at a difficulty of zero. Another 1,000 compare Superwhisper and Wispr Flow directly. Those people are about to pay for a dictation app, or already are and want a better one. Right now Medium posts and YouTube videos get them.

The existing pages can stay mostly as they are. I'd keep the vs pages. Then I'd add alternatives pages that answer "what should I use instead of Wispr Flow", and a page comparing the two leaders that's honest about both (and makes the case for a third option).

The bigger gap is the searches it doesn't target at all: non-brand searches that Wispr Flow, Superwhisper, Willow Voice, Spokenly, Aqua Voice and Typeless rank for in the US (Ahrefs), with no matching page in Monologue's sitemap.

Most of the competitors' non-brand traffic lands on a handful of pages, and none of them sells anything directly.

### 1. A best dictation apps page

The biggest cluster is people choosing a dictation app: about 8,850 US searches a month across nine searches, at difficulties from 5 to 43.

Wirecutter, Zapier and PCMag rank for them, next to the lists from Willow Voice and Superwhisper. Zapier's list already includes Monologue. PCMag's, Willow Voice's and Superwhisper's don't.

Monologue has something none of these lists publish: measured accuracy. Its post introducing mono-1 tested its own model on WildSpeech-Bench (a 7.22% word error rate, second-lowest of the systems tested, behind GPT-4o Transcribe at 6.68%). Run every app on the list through the same test, and publish the error rates and response times in one table. Be as honest about where other apps win as the comparison pages already are.

## Get the changes seen quickly

Every step above either changes existing pages or adds new ones, and Google doesn't recrawl a two-week-old comparison page on your schedule. I'd submit each new and changed URL to [Index My Site](https://indexmysite.app/) the day it ships, so Google is asked to recrawl it straight away instead of finding it on its own schedule. That matters most for the retitled comparison pages, where the old title is what Google currently has.

## How I ran this

Everything in this teardown came from SEO Skill, run by an agent. You can run the same audit on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free. You sign in with Google, and there's no password and nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web work too. The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for each one.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. The key gives your agent search volumes, rankings and results pages, like the tables above.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine, so it can read any site:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
Audit example.com and every site we own.
Crawl each one with seo crawl. Find where our sites
link to each other and which pages those links hit.
Use seo_research to find the searches our buyers
make, who ranks for them and whether we can compete.
Find the searches our competitors win that we have
no page for. Ask ai_answers ten questions our buyers
ask, for us and our top competitor.
Rank every fix by how close it sits to revenue.
```

On your own site, the hosted server adds the part I couldn't see from the outside: your real Search Console clicks and queries. With those, you get measured numbers where this post has estimates.

Then crawl your parent or sister sites and look at where they link to you. Most audits skip that step.
