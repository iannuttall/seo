[Typefully](https://typefully.com/) is a tool for writing and scheduling posts on X, LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon and Instagram. It makes money from subscriptions, and it has an API and an MCP server, so AI agents can draft and schedule posts too. Fabrizio Rinaldi and Francesco Di Lorenzo built it.

Its site has a Domain Rating of 74 and about 8,200 linking sites, free tools that already bring most of its search traffic, and scheduling data from more than 10,000 customers. That's enough to rank for the searches people make before they pay for a scheduler. Four changes would do most of the work, in order of what they'd bring:

1. Turn the "best time to post" pages into a study built on Typefully's own scheduling data.
2. Make the LinkedIn and Threads scheduler pages answer the search, and link the LinkedIn text formatter to them.
3. Publish a page per platform for the MCP server, which AI answers now credit to Buffer.
4. Tidy up the public profile pages, so only real users' profiles are indexed.

The tools are the asset to build on. The LinkedIn text formatter ranks fourth for "linkedin text formatter" (4,800 US searches a month) and gets about 7,400 visits a month, and the people using it write LinkedIn posts for a living, so they're Typefully's buyers. The AI writing tools bring more visits, but the searches behind them ("ai text generator", "free ai writer") come from people who want a paragraph written for free. The gap is non-brand search: about 3% of all visits land on a page that sells scheduling, so the people who need a scheduler and don't know Typefully yet mostly don't find it.

## 4. Housekeeping: public profiles

Anyone can sign up for Typefully and get a public profile at typefully.com/their-username. Ahrefs finds 31 profile and post pages from 24 accounts that rank for searches about gambling and escort sites, mostly in Vietnam and Korea. Together they get about 2,500 visits a month.

These rankings come and go. On 15 October, Ahrefs credited a profile for a TikTok download site with about 175,000 visits a month, for its own name in Pakistan, Nigeria, Chile and the UK.

**The fix:**

- Add `noindex` to profile and shared post pages by default. Let a profile be indexed only when its owner opts in and the account has published through Typefully. Real users lose little: other public profiles and posts get about 1,300 visits a month between them.
- Mark every bio link and every link in a shared post `rel="ugc nofollow"`. [Google asks sites to do this](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links) for links that users add.
- Return a 404 for usernames that don't exist.
- Delete the 24 accounts, and add a sign-up check for gambling and escort terms in usernames and bios.

To confirm it worked, crawl the old spam addresses with `seo crawl --mode list` and check each one returns a 404 or carries `noindex`. Ahrefs should drop them from Typefully's top pages within a few weeks.

The homepage has a smaller version of the same problem: its text is in the HTML, but none of its 64 links are.

## How I ran this

Everything in this teardown came from SEO Skill, run by an agent. You can run the same audit on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free. You sign in with Google, and there's no password and nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web work too. The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for each one.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. The key gives your agent search volumes, rankings and results pages, like the tables above. With DataForSEO it can also ask ChatGPT and Google's AI answers your questions and check who they cite.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine, so it can read any site:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
Audit example.com. Find where our search traffic
lands and how much of it reaches a page that sells.
Crawl the site with seo crawl and check what a
made-up address and a user profile page return.
Use seo_research to find the searches our buyers
make, who ranks and which of our pages should.
Ask ai_answers 10 buyer questions for us and our
top competitor, and say who gets cited.
Rank the fixes and give me the few that matter most.
```

On your own site, the hosted server adds the part I couldn't see from the outside: your real Search Console clicks and queries. With those, you get measured numbers where this post has estimates.

If your product has public user pages, request a few of them as a stranger would. That's where the spam profiles in this teardown turned up.
