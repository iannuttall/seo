In 2023 I [shared two regex filters](https://x.com/iannuttall/status/1643246625442222087) for finding long-tail keywords in Search Console, and the post had 79,000 views. I still use them for the same two jobs: improving the page that already ranks, and finding topics worth their own post. These days I ask my agent to run the filters for me.

A long, specific search your page already shows for tells you what a reader wanted from that page. Improving the page for them gives it a better chance to move up for that search and the ones around it. When several of those searches point at a topic the page doesn't cover, that's your next post.

## Start with the searches at positions 4 to 20

I start with searches at average positions 4 to 20, because Google already shows the page for them. On 24 of my sites over the 28 days to 10 November 2026, those positions held a median 53% of each site's listed searches and 62% of its listed impressions.

<AgentStep setup prompt="Which searches does example.com show for at positions 4 to 20 in Search Console? Group them by page, then read each page and tell me which searches it doesn't answer yet." />

The agent keeps the rows at positions 4 to 20 and reads each page to judge what's missing. The report also has two ready-made lists: `quick-wins` for searches at positions 4 to 10 that get fewer clicks than your site's usual rate, and `second-page` for pages at positions 11 to 20 with their searches.

## Use regex to pull out long searches and questions

Search Console's query filter takes regular expressions: click + Add filter, choose Query, then Custom (regex). These are the two I use.

This one finds searches of seven words or more:

```text
^(\S+\s+){6,}\S+$
```

This one finds searches that start with a question word:

```text
^(what|why|where|when|how|who|which|can|does|is|are|should)\b
```

Your agent can run them with the page beside each search:

<AgentStep prompt="Show me every Google search with seven or more words that example.com appears for, with the page, impressions and position. Then do the same for searches that start with a question word." />

## Expect a short list, because Google hides most of the long tail

Long searches are a small share of what Search Console lists. On the same 24 sites, searches of seven words or more were a median 6% of listed searches and 2.7% of listed impressions. Searches starting with a question word were a median 8% of listed searches and 5% of listed impressions.

Most of the rest are anonymized queries, which Google keeps out of the list to protect privacy. On these 24 sites, a median 63% of each site's impressions came from searches Search Console didn't name. That's a slightly different set of sites from my Bing post, where it was 59%. My post on [the long-tail searches Search Console hides](/blog/bing-long-tail) shows how Bing's query report fills in many of them.

Across the 24 sites there were 4,302 searches of seven words or more at positions 4 to 20. Most got only a few impressions each, and 3,885 of them (90%) got no clicks in the 28 days. Each one is small, but together they show what readers wanted from your pages.

## Decide what each search needs from the page

Read each long search as a reader telling you what they hoped to find. Then decide what would serve them, in this order:

1. If the search is part of the page's topic, give the page that ranks a better section that answers it. Use the reader's words in the heading where they read naturally.
2. If it's a small follow-up question a reader of that page would actually ask, answer it in an FAQ at the end.
3. If several searches point at a topic nothing on your site covers, write a new post on that topic, and link to it from the page that showed for them. Expanding into topics readers already search for is how I grow a site. Write one post per topic, not one per search. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) call it scaled content abuse when many pages are made "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users".

## How this was checked

- The figures come from 24 of the Search Console sites I run, over the 28 days from 14 October to 10 November 2026, using Google's final data and web search only.
- I kept sites with at least 1,000 impressions and left out sites whose query list hit the 25,000 rows the API returns in one call, because their shares would be incomplete.
- Shares of listed searches and impressions count only the searches Search Console names. The anonymized share is each site's total impressions minus the impressions in its listed searches.
- Searches were counted by words separated by spaces, and questions by the same question-word filter as above.
