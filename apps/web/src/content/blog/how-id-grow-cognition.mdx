Cognition makes Devin, an AI coding agent, and Devin Desktop, the IDE it called Windsurf until 2 June 2026. It sells plans from free to Enterprise, and its biggest deals are enterprise work such as COBOL and .NET migrations.

On 23 September 2026 Cognition posted an [SEO & AEO listing](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/cognition/7a9d995f-bc55-4347-9db3-1d366308a95e) on its jobs board, and it was still open on 6 November 2026. It asks for someone to "build Cognition's organic demand engine from the ground up... across Devin, Windsurf, and our broader web presence". This post is what I'd do in that job.

It uses only public pages, search data, AI answers and Cognition's own posts, which anyone can see from outside.

Key findings:

- Cognition's search traffic is split across five hosts, with about 63,689 estimated Google visits a month between them (Ahrefs, November 2026). Cursor gets about 398,182.
- Those hosts have up to 74,752 linking sites between them, against Cursor's 62,594. The authority is there; it's spread thin.
- About 96% of the estimated traffic from Cognition's top 150 US keywords is brand search (DataForSEO). Devin wasn't in the top 27 for "ai coding agent" (8,100 US searches a month) in either of two live checks.
- AI answers named Devin in 19 of 30 answers to coding agent questions, and in 0 of 18 answers about COBOL and codebase migrations, the work its enterprise customers buy.
- windsurf.com has redirected to devin.ai since June 2026, but 18 of the 245 pages in its sitemap still answer 200, and every made-up address returns 200 with "This page does not exist".

## Five hosts become two: devin.ai for Devin, cognition.com for the company

The listing asks for a "technical SEO roadmap across site architecture, crawlability, indexing, internal linking, structured data, page performance, redirects, canonicalization". All of those start with the same decision about which host Google and AI answers should treat as Devin's, and which as the company's.

Each host has built its own links, and today most of those links reach Devin through a redirect.

I'd make devin.ai the home of everything Devin. That means product, pricing, docs, the product blog and every page that sells. It has the highest Domain Rating of the set (84, [Domain Rating by Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/)), it already holds the pricing page and the 173 moved Windsurf posts, and it's the name buyers search for ("devin" gets 22,200 US searches a month, DataForSEO). Every Windsurf host folds into it.

cognition.com stays as the company site, the way Anthropic runs anthropic.com next to claude.com. anthropic.com keeps the research and company news, and anthropic.com/pricing redirects to claude.com/pricing (checked 6 November 2026). For Cognition that means research, FrontierCode, careers and company news on cognition.com, and anything a buyer would act on at devin.ai.

The split only works if each launch has one home. Today two blogs announce product launches. Devin Desktop was announced on cognition.com and Devin Review 2.0 on devin.ai. Product launches belong on devin.ai, with a short company post on cognition.com that links to them.

**The fix, in order:**

1. Build the URL map first. It lists every URL in each old sitemap, every URL Ahrefs shows with links or traffic, and every address in the Wayback Machine, each with one final address on devin.ai or cognition.com.
2. Move the content to its new home and check each page renders with a self canonical.
3. Switch the redirects on, one rule per host, each going straight to its final page in one hop.
4. Update the links Cognition controls. These are the 598 blog links between its own hosts (172 of them pass through a redirect today and 20 end in a 404), the X and LinkedIn profiles, and the JSON-LD.
5. Submit the new sitemaps and use Search Console's Change of Address tool for each old domain.

To confirm it worked, crawl the URL map with `seo crawl --mode list`. Every old address should answer with a single 301 to a 200 page.

## How I ran this

Everything in this teardown came from SEO Skill, run by an agent, plus the Ahrefs MCP for top pages and traffic history. You can run the same audit on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free, you sign in with Google, and there's nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. It gives your agent search volumes, rankings, results pages and AI answers.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
Audit every host we own, including old and
redirected domains. Follow each redirect and
check what unknown addresses return.
Crawl each host with seo crawl and list the
links between our hosts that redirect or 404.
Use seo_research for the category, comparison
and migration searches our buyers make.
Ask ai_answers 16 buyer questions for us and
for our biggest competitor.
Rank every fix by how close it sits to pipeline.
```

If you run more than one domain, list every host first and follow every redirect. In this teardown, the biggest fixes were addresses nobody had looked at since the rebrand.
