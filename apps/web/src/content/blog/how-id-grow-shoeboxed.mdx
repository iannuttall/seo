[Shoeboxed](https://www.shoeboxed.com/) turns receipts into tax records. Small business owners snap receipts with the app, forward them by email, or stuff the paper ones into a prepaid Magic Envelope and mail it. Shoeboxed scans them, has a person check the data, and files each one by tax category. The same app tracks mileage in the background.

It makes money from subscriptions, from $9 a month with a 30-day money-back guarantee. It was founded in 2007, and its pricing page says more than 552,000 small businesses have used it. Doug Breaker owns and runs it, and he asked me how I'd grow it, adding "We have a DR of 73." Ahrefs agrees: a Domain Rating of 73, from about 3,750 referring domains. That's the same as MileIQ and more than Everlance (68), two of the mileage apps it competes with.

Its buyers are sole proprietors, 1099 contractors and small business owners who file a Schedule C, plus the bookkeepers who do it for them. Their searches are easy to name: what's the mileage rate, what can I deduct, how do I keep receipts, and which app should I use.

Two things happened. The first was deliberate: Shoeboxed removed or merged posts that had nothing to do with receipts for a business, such as store return policies and accounting jokes. Those posts brought shoppers, not buyers, and Ahrefs finds almost no links to them, so cutting them cost little.

The second was a ranking drop. The Excel expense template post still exists, and it ranked third for "expense report template excel" in September 2025. Today it's outside the top 15, behind Microsoft and Smartsheet. The Google Sheets tracker went from third to eighth for "expense tracker google sheets".

The good news is in the footer. Shoeboxed has spent 2026 building free tools for the biggest searches its buyers make, and they haven't started ranking yet.

## The four changes that bring most of the gain

1. Get the new IRS mileage rate calculator ranking. "irs mileage rate 2026" gets 109,000 US searches a month, and pages with 2 to 7 referring domains get 4,600 to 7,500 visits a month from it.
2. Finish the Schedule C and per diem sections. "schedule c" gets 66,000 US searches a month at a difficulty of 14, and the Schedule C index page returns a 404.
3. Turn the AI Overview citation for "receipt scanner app" into organic rankings, with comparison pages and a business card scanning page.
4. Let Google and AI answers read the help center. All 49 help articles are marked noindex.

Then a few smaller fixes and a links plan to help all of it rank.

Shoeboxed's page already has what these pages have, and more. It opens with the answer ("The 2026 IRS business mileage rate is 72.5¢ a mile from January through June, and 76¢ a mile from July 1 on"), then a calculator, a free PDF for your accountant, every rate since 2017, and a section on how far 158 Shoeboxed drivers actually drive. It's signed by Doug and dated 26 October.

It's also new. The Wayback Machine has no copy of it, Google has indexed it, and Ahrefs finds it ranking for nothing yet. A new page on a 73 site still needs a reason for Google to put it above pages that have held these spots all year, and today it has no links from other sites and a few links from Shoeboxed's own footer.

**The fix:**

1. Make it the mileage hub. Link to it with the words "2026 IRS mileage rate" from the mileage tracker page, the gig driver and truck owner-operator pages, the mileage log template and the Canadian rate post, which already ranks sixth for "cra mileage rate 2026".
2. Keep one address for every year. People still search for last year's rate when they file (37,000 searches for "irs mileage rate 2025"), and the page already has the history. Update the title and the first sentence when the rate changes, and keep the URL.
3. Be first with the 2027 rate. The IRS usually announces it in December. Update the page that day and send it to Index My Site, so the page with the new number is the one Google sees first.
4. Give writers something to link to. The 158 drivers section is the start of a report (see the links section below).

To confirm it worked, check Search Console for impressions on "irs mileage rate 2026" and "mileage rate 2026" within two weeks of the links going live.

### The mileage tracker itself

Mileage is a paid feature too, and the app searches are harder. "mileage tracker app" gets 2,400 US searches a month at a difficulty of 45, and "free mileage tracker app" 1,300. In three AI answers to "best mileage tracker app for self employed", Shoeboxed wasn't named once. Everlance, MileIQ and TripLog were.

Shoeboxed's mileage tracker page leads with "The Mileage Tracking App That Does The Work For You". The difference its own llms.txt names is the better pitch: receipts and mileage in one app. That's the line for the title and the first sentence, and the reason for two comparison pages, Shoeboxed vs MileIQ and Shoeboxed vs Everlance, for people who pay for one app to track miles and another for receipts.

## 4. The help center: 49 answers Google is told to skip

All 49 help center pages in the crawl are marked noindex, from the index to articles like "How do I integrate with QuickBooks Online?" and "How do I import my business card contacts into Microsoft Outlook?".

These are the questions people check before they pay: does it connect to QuickBooks Online, Xero, Salesforce or Dropbox, and how does Gmail receipt sync work. Bookkeepers ask them most. In AI answers to "best receipt app for bookkeepers and accountants", Shoeboxed was named in neither of the two answers shown, and Dext, Hubdoc and AutoEntry were cited for their integration pages.

**The fix:** remove the noindex from the help articles and add a canonical to each, keep it only on pages for signed-in customers, and link the integration articles from the features and pricing pages. The bookkeeper partner page (Shoeboxed's case study of a bookkeeper who saves 900 hours a year is the proof for it) is noindex too. If it's the public offer for bookkeepers, it should be indexable.

To confirm it worked, check the help articles' indexed count in Search Console a week later.

## Four technical fixes

### 1. The homepage FAQ markup doesn't parse

The homepage has FAQ structured data, and it breaks on a raw line break inside one of the answers, so a parser reads nothing from it. The crawl found invalid JSON-LD on 23 pages.

<BeforeAfter
  caption="The homepage's FAQ JSON-LD today and written with a JSON encoder."
  before={{ label: "Today", html: beforeFaq }}
  after={{ label: "Fixed", html: afterFaq }}
/>

**The fix:** build the JSON-LD with `to_json` in the Rails view instead of writing the answer HTML into the string. Google no longer shows FAQ rich results for most sites, but other search engines and AI crawlers read the markup. To confirm, run the 23 pages through a crawl and check for zero invalid blocks.

### 2. Blog links to pricing carry tracking tags

Blog posts link to the pricing and features pages with `utm_` tags, and some link to /home instead of the homepage. Each tagged address is a separate URL with the same content, and a tag on an internal link overwrites the visit's source in Google Analytics, so sign-ups that started from Google get credited to a "blog" campaign.

<BeforeAfter
  caption="Links from Shoeboxed's blog posts to its own pages, today and without tags."
  before={{ label: "Today", html: beforeUtm }}
  after={{ label: "Fixed", html: afterUtm }}
/>

**The fix:** plain internal links, and a 301 from /home to the homepage.

### 3. The sitemap has no dates and leads with college costs

The sitemap lists 2,514 addresses with no lastmod on any of them. 1,994 are 529 college cost pages, and the mileage calculator is one line among them. When the rate changes in December, nothing in the sitemap tells Google which page changed.

**The fix:** a sitemap index with one file per section (product pages, tools, mileage and per diem, Schedule C, blog, colleges), each with a real lastmod. To confirm, check the sitemap report in Search Console for each file's discovered and indexed counts.

### 4. No sharing cards

589 of the 594 pages in the crawl have no Open Graph title, description or image, so a link to the mileage calculator shared on X, LinkedIn or Slack shows as a bare address. That matters for the December report more than for rankings. Add the three tags to the layout, with the page's own title and an image per tool.

## Housekeeping

A login page for a staff support tool, on its own subdomain, ranks fifth for "shoeboxed login" (Ahrefs credits it with about 5 visits a month). Customers looking for their account end up there. Add a noindex and a robots.txt rule on that subdomain.

The 529 college cost section is 79% of the sitemap and brings about 505 visits a month, about 5% of the total, from parents pricing colleges. It doesn't have to go, but it shouldn't be what Google spends its time on before the mileage and tax pages.

## How I ran this

Everything in this teardown came from SEO Skill, run by an agent. You can run the same audit on your own site.

**1. Connect the hosted MCP server.** It's free. You sign in with Google, and there's no password and nothing to install. In Claude Code:

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http seoskill https://seoskill.dev/mcp
```

Codex, Cursor and Claude on the web work too. The [MCP guide](/docs/mcp) has the steps for each one.

**2. Add a research key.** On your [account page](/account), save an Ahrefs or DataForSEO key. The key gives your agent search volumes, rankings, the link gap and AI answers, like the tables above.

**3. Install the CLI for crawling.** The crawl runs on your machine, so it can read any site:

```sh
npm i -g seo
seo login
```

Shoeboxed sits behind Cloudflare, which answered a fast crawl with a 429 after 12 pages. A slower crawl read 600:

```sh
seo crawl https://www.example.com --max-pages 600 \
  --concurrency 2 --fetch-interval-cap 1 --fetch-interval-ms 1200
```

**4. Ask your agent.** This is close to the prompt I used:

```text
Audit example.com. Crawl it with seo crawl, slowly
if it rate-limits. Find the pages we built for searches
we don't rank for yet, and say what each one needs.
Use seo_research to find the searches our buyers make,
who ranks for them and whether we can compete.
Run link_gap against our three biggest competitors.
Ask ai_answers ten buyer questions for us and for
our biggest competitor.
Rank every fix by how close it sits to revenue.
```

On your own site, the hosted server adds the part I couldn't see from the outside: your real Search Console clicks and queries. With those, you get measured numbers where this post has estimates.
